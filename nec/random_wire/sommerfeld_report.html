The near-ground Sommerfeld failure is the method, not the port

Six NEC-2 builds and NEC-4.2 against a limit with an exact answer. The two somnec.c fixes on nec2c's validation branch are real and measurable — they move nec2c onto the FORTRAN to five figures — but they do not touch this regime, because the FORTRAN misses it too, and NEC-4.2 does not. Then a second measurement, at the conductivity of real soil, ranks the same codes differently, and only NEC-4.2 comes out of both intact.

What this reports

  1. Below about 0.05 wavelengths the implementations split two ways, not along a spectrum: NEC-4.2 and nec2++ give one answer, and nec2c, the validation branch, nec2dx and aegnec2 give another, agreeing with each other to three or four figures across five orders of magnitude of error.
  2. NEC-4.2 reaches the limit, and reaches it best — +0.05% at 0.02 wl where the NEC-2 lineage is +91.9%. That also answers the obvious objection to this test: if sigma 1e10 were merely a numerical stress case outside the method's envelope, NEC-4.2 would struggle with it too.
  3. The validation fixes work as designed. They move nec2c within the FORTRAN group, onto nec2dx. They do not move it toward the limit, and nothing in that branch should have been expected to.
  4. At necpp master, both of those source sites read correctly — offered as an observation, since I do not know the history between the two codebases and cannot say whether they were ever otherwise.
  5. nec2dxs segfaults on Sommerfeld ground below 0.5 wl, dying in the ground setup rather than returning a wrong number.
  6. At real soil the ranking reverses. With the fed element well up and only a counterpoise near the ground, nec2c tracks NEC-4.2 to 1% down to a centimetre while nec2++ falls 50% away. Passing the limit does not predict behaviour at the conductivity of actual earth.

The test

As a soil's conductivity rises, a lossy half-space becomes a perfect conductor. So GN 2 at sigma 1e10 must reproduce what GN 1 gives for the same geometry. That is exact physics rather than a modelling judgement, every implementation computes the GN 1 side identically, and the correct value of the gap is therefore zero everywhere.

The deck is a horizontal half-wave dipole, centre fed, 11 segments, 145.9 MHz, swept in height. Each cell below is the feedpoint resistance under GN 2 against the same geometry under GN 1, as a percentage.

Reading it

The validation column sits inside the FORTRAN group, and the small distance it moved is the whole story about those two somnec.c commits. Master sits slightly off nec2dx and the branch lands on it — at 0.01 wl, -95.35% becomes -95.08% against the FORTRAN's -95.09%. That is exactly what a corrected transcription should do, and it is a good argument for the branch on its own terms. It is also why the branch cannot close this gap: the target it converges on is itself 95% from the known answer.

NEC-4.2 is what makes that reading safe. It is an independent implementation with a ground treatment reworked precisely for this regime, and it reaches the limit from the other side of the split — closer to zero than nec2++ at every height below 0.05 wl. Two implementations that share no code agree, and the five that descend from the NEC-2 FORTRAN agree with each other instead.

It also disposes of the strongest objection available to this test, which is that sigma 1e10 is far outside anything physical and might be probing numerical conditioning rather than the method. If that were what the table showed, NEC-4.2 would be in trouble too. It returns +0.05% at 0.02 wl.

Same result on a second geometry, and still at the limit. An end-fed 32.6 m wire 9.1 m up with a 7.6 m return lying 5 cm off the soil, at 7.15 MHz, sigma 1000: nec2++ 1442.1 Ω against a perfect-ground 1441.7, so it converges. nec2c master reads 1867.6, the branch 1868.3, and nec2dx 1868.4, against a perfect-ground 1441.5 — 29.6% high, all three. Sigma 1000 is the operative words: the next section runs the same antenna at the conductivity of real soil and the ranking does not survive.

At the conductivity of real soil, the ranking reverses

The limit is a diagnostic, chosen because it has an exact answer. It is not the regime anyone models. Running the same end-fed geometry at the soil constants a real installation sits on — and with the fed element where a real end fed has it, 0.22 wavelengths up, so that only the counterpoise is near the ground:

nec2c holds to one percent of NEC-4.2 all the way down; nec2++ falls to half. That is the opposite of the table above, where nec2c is 91% out at 0.02 wl and nec2++ is fine.

The two are not in conflict, and the difference between them is what to take away. NEC-2's Sommerfeld failure needs the fed element near the interface, which is what the dipole sweep puts there. A counterpoise near the interface, with the source well up, is a far milder case and NEC-2 handles it. So the dipole test identifies a real defect and does not predict where it bites.

Which leaves NEC-4.2 as the only one of the three correct in both regimes, and it is the reason to prefer it — rather than the limit test alone, which would have recommended nec2++ and been wrong by a factor of two for this antenna.

What necpp's source reads at master

Both fixes carry the same sentence in their commit messages — a475aad, the gshank L1-norm correction, and 3f16cbc, the evlua contour correction:

The slip lives only in the NEC lineage (nec2c to necpp and xnec2c); nec2dx.f to nec2dxs is unaffected.

The nec2dx half matches what I measured: it behaves as its own lineage rather than as nec2c's. On the necpp half I can only report what the file says today. At master (a8e829e) the convergence gate in src/c_evlcom.cpp takes the two magnitudes separately: 

amg=fabs(real(a1))+fabs(imag(a1));

and evlua selects its tail integration with a plain if/else on a bool jump, so exactly one of the two branches always runs.

I have not traced how these two codebases relate, whether these sites were ever different in necpp, or what xnec2c reads — anyone who knows that history will place this faster than I can. The reason it is here at all is narrow: it means necpp's reaching the limit is not accounted for by these two sites, so whatever separates the two columns is something else, still unidentified.

nec2dxs crashes rather than diverging

The Fortran build from yeti01/nec2 solves 0.5 wl and then segfaults, with a core dump, at every height below it. It dies immediately after printing the ground constants, so the failure is in the Sommerfeld setup and not in the solve: 

RELATIVE DIELECTRIC CONST.= 13.000
CONDUCTIVITY= 1.000E+10 MHOS/METER
Segmentation fault (core dumped)

Worth separating from the rest: it is the only build here that fails loudly instead of returning a confident wrong number.

What I am not claiming

  • Not that any of them is right at the bottom. necpp holds to roughly 0.005 wl and then goes the same way, reaching +125.70% at 0.002 wl; even NEC-4.2 is +50.53% there. The split is about where each stops working, not about one being correct everywhere.
  • Not that I know why necpp differs. I established that it passes and that it lacks these two slips. I did not identify what in its ground evaluation accounts for the difference, and someone who knows that code will get there faster.
  • Not that PyNEC is a third witness. It wraps this same nec2++, so it is excluded above; it was run, and agrees with a locally built binary to every figure printed. The two independent codes on that side are NEC-4.2 and nec2++.
  • Not that passing the limit proves correctness over real soil. The test only exercises the high-conductivity corner, where an exact answer exists. That was a caveat when this was written and is now measured: nec2++ passes the limit and is a factor of two out at a centimetre over average soil. Passing is necessary rather than sufficient, and there is still no oracle at real soil — NEC-4.2 is preferred there on its record in both regimes, not on a proof.

Reproducing it

One script drives every solver over the same generated decks, taking each as name=style:path, where the style is how that implementation wants to be invoked — flags for nec2c's -i/-o, attached for nec2++, stdio, jobname for aegnec2, and prompt for NEC-4, which asks for the two file names in turn. --decks=DIR writes the 16 decks out if you would rather run them by hand. The NEC-4.2 column needs a licensed copy; every other column can be built from source. 

git clone https://github.com/KJ7LNW/nec2c && cd nec2c
git checkout validation && ./autogen.sh && ./configure && make
# builds ./nec2c and, on this branch, the FORTRAN ./nec2dx

python sommerfeld_cross.py \
    nec4.2=prompt:/usr/bin/nec4d42 \
    nec2++=attached:$NECPP/bin/nec2++ \
    nec2c=$STOCK/nec2c \
    nec2c-val=$VALIDATION/nec2c \
    nec2dx=$VALIDATION/nec2dx \
    nec2dxs=stdio:$NEC2/nec2dxs \
    aegnec2=jobname:$AEGNEC2/bin/aegnec2

A single deck, if that is all you want — this is the 0.02 wl case:
